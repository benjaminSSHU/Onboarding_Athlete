# Prototype Testing Session: Feedback Categorization

Here is a categorized breakdown of the feedback and insights gathered during the prototype testing session. The conversation highlights a classic product design dynamic: balancing the designer's structural vision with the user's need for flexibility and clear next steps.

### 1. Goal Setting & Personalization
This was the most heavily debated section, focusing on how to capture user ambitions without boxing them in.

* **Rigid vs. Flexible Goals:**
    * *Context:* The screen "Quel résultat veux-tu atteindre en premier ?" offered specific, pre-written goals (e.g., "Courir 10 km en 10 semaines" or "Atteindre 55 ml/kg/min de VO2max").
    * *Feedback:* Benjamin strongly pushed back against this. He found the options too niche (most people don't know their VO2 max target) and too restrictive. If a user wants to run 10 km in 3 weeks, a 10-week option feels irrelevant. 
    * *Proposed Solution:* They agreed to pivot to a "Mad Libs" style "fill-in-the-blanks" system (inspired by the app *Atoms*). The user will construct their own sentence: "I want to `[Action]` `[Value]` in `[Timeframe]`".

### 2. UI/UX & Form Inputs
Feedback on how information is gathered and the visual cues used to guide the user.

* **Adding Visual Warmth:**
    * *Context:* The "Quel est ton niveau ?" screen. 
    * *Feedback:* Benjamin liked the inclusion of emojis alongside the text options, noting that it makes the interface feel friendlier and less clinical.
* **List Limitations:**
    * *Context:* The "Quel sport pratiques-tu le plus ?" screen showed only four options (Course, Vélo, Aviron, Mixte).
    * *Feedback:* Benjamin immediately flagged this as too limited. Even if tailored to a specific "cluster" of users, people have diverse hobbies. He suggested using a drop-down menu (liste déroulante) to allow for a wider array of sports.
* **Brand vs. Object:**
    * *Context:* The connected devices screen.
    * *Feedback:* While the question asks for a "connected object," the answers list brands (Apple Watch, Garmin, Whoop). Benjamin suggested that adding logos or icons would make this screen much faster to scan.

### 3. Wording & Consistency
Small phrasing choices that have a big impact on user comprehension.

* **The "Daily" Contradiction:**
    * *Context:* The screen asking to set a "petite habitude quotidienne" (small daily habit), followed by a slider asking how many days a week they want to do it.
    * *Feedback:* Benjamin pointed out the logical flaw: if a user sets the slider to 3 days a week, it is no longer a "daily" habit. 
    * *Proposed Solution:* Drop the word "quotidienne" and use a more flexible term like "habitude régulière."
* **Actionable vs. Negative Results:**
    * *Context:* Discussing the results of the "Fitness Test" (the red/yellow/green dashboard). 
    * *Feedback:* Benjamin noted that the wording was too diagnostic. Instead of just pointing out a user's weak point (e.g., a red light for "Respiration"), the copy should be an actionable, positive directive: "Améliorer votre contrôle respiratoire".

### 4. Flow Logic & The "Aha!" Moment
This feedback focused on the final step of the onboarding and whether it successfully capitalizes on the momentum built during the flow.

* **The Missing Call-to-Action (CTA):**
    * *Context:* The final "Ton profil est prêt" dashboard.
    * *Feedback:* This was a major realization for both of them. The user just spent minutes carefully defining a new micro-habit (e.g., 1 minute of planking, anchored after their morning coffee). However, the final screen presents them with competing, unrelated actions: "Find a coach" or "Take a fitness test." 
    * *The Issue:* There is no immediate CTA to actually *execute* or track the habit they just built. The onboarding builds intent, but the final screen drops the ball by not giving them a clear, unified next step related to what they just told the app they wanted to do.
